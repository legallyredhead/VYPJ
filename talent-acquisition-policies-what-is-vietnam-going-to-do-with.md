BG of the policy:

Chính sách thu hút nhân tài là gì?

Talent Acquisition policies are regulations that are published to attract talented young minds for: 1, Contributing to the country’s public services, giving everyone a chance to achieve personal and professional development. 2, Compete and/or integrate in the global market (such as RnD, inventions, strategies, …) 3, Ensure that the next leaders of the young generations will be promising through training, nurturing strong will and earn opportunities to lead and develop the country.

Factors that make up a Talent Acquisition Policy:

Working environment: Working conditions, equipment, facilities, specific environmental elements such as noise, light, policies such as guidance, appreciation and empowerment, pressure, choice, challenges, opportunities … This could affect: Satisfaction, achieve flow in work, comfortable in workspace, Positivity in work, empowerment and motivation to keep working → Psychological climate to feel safe and comfortable.

Remuneration: Pay system, can differ to give uniqueness, can determine job attractiveness, convey information about the organization’s values, culture, philosophy, …

Rewards: To enforce employment relationships, retain employees. Including employees benefit (assets, security, insurance, medical offers), performance and financial recognition (Cash bonus, enforce individualism and independence for motivation)

Training: Beneficial for personal and professional development. Offer access to training for organizational (and even national) growth → Improvements, engage in career challenges, increase retention rate.

Career advancement: Planning to maintain skills in the market, flexibility in job and life options.

Significance

The case in Vietnam is simply too obvious: Brain drain, talented people are all going out abroad, while those who stayed do not live up to expectations (such as being lazy, corrupted, …) → The Talent Acquisition policies are important to retain talented students and high-skills workers in the country while also provide trainings for skills that are suitable and can benefit the country

Why isn’t it working for Vietnam? The decrees are not being institutionalized, not synchronized and not suitable for all regions and the condition of each institute. To focus on earning degrees (bachelor, master, PhD), examinations are not suitable and focusing too much on theories rather than critical thinking, adaptability and professional skills. The jobs that talented people receive are not according to their profession and skills, and expected them to be good leaders (when they actually are not) and did not give them the remuneration they desire. Payment system is not suitable because they are for office workers and not consultants or professionals → Unable to keep up with the daily need, no motivation made, many moved to private sectors. Working conditions are too poor or insufficient for research programs and creativity, and not flexible enough → Unproductive, not cooperating, resign.

Objective

Strategies

Schools (Esp Head of School): Establish Consultation department, set out rules and system to provide service for students. Survey and analyze data of psychological issues/ concerns from students to observe, follow and update. Conclude and report to head departments on a regular period.

Facilities: Implement this circular in all schools under the control. Instruct, check up and observe the progress. Ministry of Education and Training: Cooperate with relevant agencies to implement, monitor and check up this circular in facilities, earn reports and organize training and certificates for teachers and consultants. Facilities specialized in training psychological consultation: Survey and organize training plans and curriculum to address the need in the region/ facility. Create necessary documents and provide certificates for teachers and consultants who met the requirements.
