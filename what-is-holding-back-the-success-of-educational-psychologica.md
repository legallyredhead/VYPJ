What is holding back the success of educational psychological consultation in Vietnam?

Abstract

The psychological counseling services at schools in Vietnam were still struggling to tackle the rising issue of prolonging disorders, stress, depression and other common mental health problems in students. According to UNICEF, reports from the governments proved that although there has been an increase in counseling activities in many provinces, still the need for such service remained high. This essay looks at Circular 31/2017/TT-BGDĐT published by the Vietnamese Ministry of Education and Training as well as other related work from UNICEF and psychological studies to evaluate the effect of actions taken by the Vietnamese government to address the alarming mental health issues. Although the circular had addressed the more significant aspects to mitigate the problems at hand, further investments and amendments could have been made to handle the issue more efficiently and quickly.

Introduction

The current mental health quality for students in Vietnam can be said with one sentence: “Improving, but not enough.” According to research taken by Dr. Hoàng Trung Học in 2022, the result of consulting with a sample of 20,000 students from all three regions of Vietnam proved the situation to be quite alarming (Sỹ, 2022). 60.5% of students went through stress in different levels, 38.1% suffered anxiety disorder and 30.4% underwent Major Depressive Disorder. Each year, there are around 36,000 to 40,000 suicidal cases related to mental health issues. These numbers got even more severe during and after the Covid-19 pandemic due to the lack of social interaction and proper educational methods. This begs a question regarding psychological consultation in schools, especially in secondary and high school in Vietnam: Aren’t there supposed to be psychological consultation facilities in Vietnamese public and private schools?

In December 2017, the Vietnamese government released Circular 31/2017/TT-BGDĐT, titled “Guidelines for Implementing Psychological Counseling for High School Students” (Thư viện Pháp luật, 2017). This circular was made to address the lack of psychological support for high school students from across all three regions of Vietnam, especially those who suffered peer pressure, heavy stress, depression, bullying and other school-related mental issues. After Covid-19, the role of counseling seems to be even more important to help students re-establish social networks, re-learn skills and recreate a sense of belonging again. Has this circular helped to reduce the impact of Vietnamese students' mental health issues? How will this circular change the high school student’s life? To answer such a question, an analysis of this circular with both advantages and drawbacks as well as some further recommendations will be made in this essay.

Circular Overview

The primary aim of Circular 31/2017/TT-BGDĐT is to provide aid for students who are suffering mental issues such as depression, stress, or disorders while studying. The role of psychological counseling according to this circular is to resolve issues and reduce the effect made by them to create a safer education environment for high school students in Vietnam. Other than that, psychologists and advisors can teach students life skills such as communication, emotional control and regulating feelings or stress, as well as nurturing a strong will, confidence and bravery. This service also aids students in determining suitable behaviors in social connection and networking, while encouraging personal growth with better mental and physical health after therapy. Circular 31/2017/TT-BGDĐT also urged to increase activity in psychological consultation in Vietnamese schools, aiming to make beneficial and positive consultation service accessible for all students.

The way to implement this circular is first, schools and especially head of schools must establish a consultation department, set out rules and systems to provide counseling services for students. Survey and analyzed data of psychological issues or concerns from students is needed to observe, follow through and update to the local government. After concluding a report, head of schools must submit it to head departments on a regular period for check up and open conferences to discuss any new occurrence of issues. Heads of the educational systems must also implement this circular in all schools under their control, and they play an important role to instruct, check up and observe the progress during the implementation process. Furthermore, schools and heads of educational systems must research and advise the People's Committee about relevant policies to aid or motivate consultants and teachers providing the service with consideration to the financial and social condition of the region. In addition, education facilities must open training sessions for consultants and teachers to make sure they have sufficient skills to consult students, as well as communicate between higher ups and the schools under supervision to quickly update progress and report any sudden problem. The Ministry of Education and Training also has to play their part in cooperating with relevant agencies to implement, monitor and check up this circular in facilities, while earning reports from education facilities and organizing training or certificates for teachers and consultants. The ministry must also integrate information regarding psychological consultation into the curriculum and activities of schools and facilities to raise students’ awareness. Another role that is mentioned in Circular 31 are facilities specialized in training psychological consultation. They must survey, organize training plans and curriculum to address the need in the region and/or facility, while also create necessary documents and provide certificates for teachers and consultants who meet the requirements.

The result was not that promising. Some schools do not have sufficient staff members who are well trained for such a role, meanwhile students are still holding back because they want to avoid meeting teachers who teach them in class as well as being afraid of being discriminated against (Mai, 2022). Policy mechanisms are not satisfactory, and some schools did not acknowledge the importance of psychological consultation, thus leading to the fact that some students receive help later than expected. Service rooms are not suitable (due to the lack of aesthetic and care, continuity, confidentiality, stability, support, or other aspects of a consulting room that plays an important role in helping students to process emotions, open up and engage attentively with the clinicians (Punzi & Singer, 2018)) or not provided, and consultants or teachers are lacking relevant documents, handbooks and guidelines. On December 22, 2021 - 3 years after implementing Circular 31 and 33, there were 40 over 53 local Ministry of Education and Training (75%) raised written plans to implement Circular 31; 35 over 53 local ministries (66%) divided works to teachers to organize psychological consultation service; and 28 over 53 (52%) built and incorporated policy framework for teachers and consultants (including reducing teaching hours for teachers who were assigned both jobs) (Báo Nhân Dân, 2023). While the numbers are promising, it is highly possible that the majority of them were big cities, while the issue of mental illness is more perturbing in the rural or less developed regions. According to a report made by UNICEF Vietnam in 2018 and 2022, in 2018, there were 12% of students in need of psychological counseling services (UNICEF Việt Nam, 2018b), but in 2022, data given by the Vietnamese government as well as private studies have concluded that the number stays the same even after 4 years, which is still alarming for the least (UNICEF Việt Nam, 2022b).

Circular 33 in 2018 was made after Circular 31 that includes instruction for social activities in schools. The Ministry of Education and Training also cooperated with global organizations such as UNICEF, Good Neighbours International (GNI), Room to Read (RtR), or national committees like Ministry of Labour - Invalids and Social Affair, the Central Executive Committee of the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union, …(Báo Nhân Dân, 2023). Psychological consultation for highschool student handbooks by famous doctors like Trần Thành Nam were also made to better guide consultants to aid students in any and all circumstances. In June 2023, the Ministry of Education and Training successfully organized training on using documents and skills for psychological consultants (which focused mainly on self-harm behavior and prevention), as well as included hotline 111 for children protection and website of the facilities to further reach students who are not available for face-to-face consultation. There were consulting groups that were established in both public and private schools, consultation services were also intertwined into extracurricular lessons, as well as weekly community gatherings that taught knowledge and skills for the major population.

Impact on Vietnamese Youth

The Circular provided accessible consultation service to help students feel safer while studying at school, and focuses specifically on preventing bullies or self harm behavior, while helping students to get through trauma and reintegrate back into their school life. Other than that, the circular pushes school consultants to teach and expose students to new soft skills, attention skills, independence, and strong will. Without this circular, schools that were struggling to implement psychological consultation services cannot address the need of improving mental health during stressful periods from students and parents, or find trouble in dealing with mental issues, bullying and suicidal cases. The lack of guidelines would also prolong the issue of long-term depression, affecting youth in learning, focusing and absorbing heavy knowledge during studies.

On a long term note, the circular helps schools to create a safe environment in which students will feel safe studying, including avoid and prevent harmful behaviors at school, reduce suicidal, depression and bully rates for students and ensure that the consultants as well as other school workers have experiences to deal with mental health issues and related causes or consequences. The circular also raises awareness about the importance of psychological consultation in school, and mitigate the high suicide and crime rate of students in Vietnam to aid the government in resolving the issue altogether. The environment without awareness about mental wellbeing will see the increase of people stereotyping seeking psychological consultation services as being crazy or having severe mental issues, otherwise they do not consider psychological counseling to be important, thus failing in addressing heavy mental issues which later can pose a major threat in any students’ life. Finally, the circular encouraged the increase of productivity in learning because students no longer have to suspend or deal with mental health issues while struggling to learn and do schoolwork, thus giving them more chances to study better, more efficiently and contribute to Vietnamese and global society.

In actual practice, there are three reports, from Cương Gián 1 primary school in Hà Tĩnh, Bình Xuân primary school in Tiền Giang and Minh Lập secondary school in Đồng Nai which detailed the progress of implementing psychological consultation in three years after Circular 31 publication. These reports are not promising, and show a major problem regarding the process of training and recruiting teachers and consultants for the service as well as opening consultation rooms.

For Minh Lập secondary school in Đồng Nai, there were no reports of counseling cases, while related activities and organization works are average without anything noteworthy (Đặng, 2021). The reports claimed that teachers who took this role were busy and the consultation process were deemed unprofessional, while also mentioned the unwillingness of students to open up for discussion. The school stated in the report that they did not have a specialized consultant nor counseling room, but the general evaluation seems superficial, the limits are unclear or were not considered, which hinted at the lack of any actual progress made for the implementation process. The solutions and future plans are not insightful - it only stated that the counselor' works will be maintained and there will be coaches for training, as well as a general mention of campaigns to spread awareness.

In Cương Gián 1 primary school, 23 cases have been reported for three years of implementation, which is quite a significant number, but in detail, 12 of such cases were disabled children who struggled with learning, 4 cases were related to health issues, 4 cases of parent consultation and 3 specific cases in which students abandoned schoolwork for games (Hoàng Thị, 2021). Through this number, it seems as though the majority of the cases which went through psychological consultation was mostly about disabled students. The report also stated that Cương Gián 1 primary school did not have sufficient staff who are well trained for psychological consultation, they are missing the consultation room and pointed out that another drawback was that students are shy and did not open themselves to seek psychological counseling. The last drawback seems to raise some questions. Children who attend primary school are still immature, they do not have the ability to comprehend nor notice subtle changes in their mental health - such as stress, depression, anxiety and the likes. It is near impossible to ask primary school students to open up for talks without reaching out to them first - which is difficult considering the number of students a teacher must manage as well as her/his knowledge of psychological health illness symptoms. Furthermore, it is important to note that there is the mention of the school’s ability to pay for consultants, training coaches or the construction of the counseling room - instead it simply states the school will provide a suitable amount of money to construct the service room. In addition, the school did not mention any solution relating to finding consultants outside of schools - which can be difficult but not impossible to not mention.

Lastly, there are 5 cases of psychological consultation reported by Bình Xuân primary school in Tiền Giang - mainly circles around gender, age and family based consultation (Nguyễn Thị, 2021). Other activities mentioned in the reports were mainly awareness-raising for the whole school, but is insufficient compared to Cương Gián 1 primary school. The report of the number of staff for psychological counseling is even more alarming - There is only one teacher who is not carefully trained and is still unfamiliar with the consulting service. This further demonstrates the lack of attention the school paid for psychological counseling, and mainly implementing this circular for its sake. The future plans were also lacking details and consideration, as there is no mention of recruiting more staff or opening counseling rooms as well as creative implementation activities for awareness-raising, instead there are only mentions of adopting further documents, training for staffs, incorporating knowledge into subjects and a few activities for school gathering.

From the data above, it proves that the actual implementation progress was met with many limits and reluctance, mainly regarding the lack of professionalism and uncooperative attitude between students and consultants. In the end, this raises a question considering what could have been better in the execution of this circular. After three years of implementation and the result was still underperformed and ineffective, it is possible that there could be more factors affecting the success of psychological consultation services, from a lack of qualified teachers and consultants, staff’s hesitance to taking in more tasks, staff’s teaching style and the ability to control a large group of students, related aspects of the education system such as study schedule, student number per classroom, workload to social issues such as family problems, prejudice and discrimination against mental illness. The major problem seen with the existing data is regarding staff’s availability, recruitment and training. Compared to the social norm, prejudice and hesitation that stems from students’ perspective, the problem of insufficient staff proved that investment and focus on it would be more realistic and effective for the long run. The implementation of psychological consultation services should be in chronological order: From recruiting staff, training staff members, opening counseling rooms to raising awareness through campaigns and educational means. Priorities must be set straight, it is not raising awareness that should be prioritized, it should have been training staff for the best consultation quality. The circular did not mention any specific orders - which is understandable considering educational centers were responsible for making detailed plans in which includes their own interpretation of orders of implementation steps, but still - the lack of proper guidelines and knowledge about implementing counseling services for students are affecting the outcome as well as attitude of the school’s management board towards the work. The government was responsible for making this clear - that it is important to have someone to listen to students’ needs and struggles to guide or heal them than only telling them that there can be danger. Furthermore, the government could have taken responsibilities to address other study quality issues to further push the process and achieve better outcomes - but it seems it will take time and effort to even notice such a situation.

Critical Analysis

This circular excels because it includes all relevant agencies and major players in providing psychological counseling services, including both public and private schools, school facilities, ministry, and specialized agencies. It also contains detailed guides and instructions on the responsibilities of each agency, including reports, organizing curriculum, training, observation and surveying, … More importantly, it included sending feedbacks and advice to the Ministry and the People’s Committee for changes and reassessment if needed, thus helps the Ministry of Education and Training to work closely with the current situation, quickly resolve problems that arise while also amend related legislation and guidelines to bring convenience and efficiency to all factors.

However, the circular should include or mention any KPI criteria for a successful implementation, while reports could be made semi-annually for faster updates or placing pressure on making progress. It should also include cooperation between consultants and heads of schools to reassess school rules to adjust them based on the data gathered from surveys, therefore placing emphasis on the role of the school executive board in creating a safe environment for students as well as raising awareness of the importance of psychological consultation.

Recommendations and Policy Alternatives

To complement Circular 31, the Ministry of Education and Training or relevant organizations such as consulting agencies and schools should consider methods to reduce stereotyping or change the mindset of parents and the community in general such as open workshops, raise awareness outside of school, encourage student-led or teacher-led projects regarding psychological wellbeing. The Ministry could amend to include such methods in this circular for students to have the courage to seek psychological consultation services without facing discrimination or shame. In addition, the circular needs to address the issue of ensuring there are sufficient staff members from each school through methods such as having minimum requirements, open channels to connect to professionals, remodeling pay and remuneration policy for staff who work both as teacher and consultant (Golden Careers Việt Nam, 2022). Funds should also be established for building facilities or for finance purposes which place emphasis on investment in psychological consultation services in school, and related policies could include a part that specifies that it is highly recommended to cooperate with national and international organizations such as UNICEF or NGOs that place focus on the mental wellbeing of students.

Conclusion

In the end, Circular 31/2017/TT-BGDĐT was necessary to improve mental wellbeing for students across Vietnam, and it has been making moderate progress with specific guidelines of communication, major roles, requirements to implement the psychological consultation service in both public and private schools from all regions. Though there should be further amendments as well as investment to address in the circular to fully cover all aspects and issues that arise, the circular had been a game changer that can potentially reduces the crime, suicide and drug abuse rates in Vietnam by influencing students to pursue a healthier lifestyle and avoid negative mental effects from their daily life as well as study journey. It is of utmost importance to provide a secure and positive environment for Vietnamese students so that the younger generations can place their focus on studying more efficiently while also caring about their own mental state.

Vietnam's youth are grappling with a silent crisis. Shockingly high rates of child abuse, coupled with a hyper-competitive education system, are fueling a surge in stress, anxiety, and depression among students. While the government acknowledges a mental health crisis, affecting an estimated 15-30% of the population, its response has been notably inadequate. The implementation of Circular 31/2017/TT-BGDĐT, aimed at bolstering these services, reveals significant shortcomings. According to a UNICEF study, approximately 26% of students face a moderate to high risk of mental health issues, yet many do not receive the necessary support, largely due to social stigma and a lack of resources.

The crisis is exacerbated by gender disparities, with girls more likely to experience emotional problems like anxiety and depression, and boys more prone to behavioral disorders. Suicidal thoughts are alarmingly common, with 21.4% of girls and 7.9% of boys contemplating suicide, and about 5.8% attempting it. These statistics highlight the inadequacy of the current school-based mental health frameworks to effectively address the needs of the student population. The academic environment in Vietnam contributes to the stress, emphasizing performance over well-being and offering few outlets like arts or sports. Students often feel disconnected from their school environment, which correlates with higher rates of mental health problems.

This VYPJ piece then critiques the circular's implementation, noting the lack of specific performance indicators and the need for a more robust approach involving all educational stakeholders. It calls for a "stepped-care" model to improve early detection and intervention, integrating mental health literacy and services into the school system more effectively.nThis piece sheds light on the systemic changes needed to better support the mental well-being of Vietnam's youth, urging for enhanced collaboration across educational, health, and social sectors.
