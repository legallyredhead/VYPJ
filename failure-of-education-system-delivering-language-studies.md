Failure of Education System: Delivering Language Studies

Abstract

This paper:

delves into a critical analysis of Vietnam's educational system, with a focus on language studies.

looks into the difficulties and flaws that prevent language education from being delivered effectively, which affects both the standard of instruction and the general results of learning.

examines the current curriculum, instructional strategies, and institutional frameworks, this study seeks to illuminate the structural shortcomings that obstruct Vietnamese students' language learning.

examines the intricate interactions between several factors that lead to the shortcomings in language teaching, including teacher preparation, resource distribution, and cultural effects.

through Decision No.2080/QD-Ttg published in 2017 by the Vietnamese Prime Minister.

Introduction

As it shapes the intellectual capital and competencies of a country's populace, education is a key factor in the socioeconomic development of that country. In Vietnam, a country experiencing rapid economic expansion and increased international integration, the effectiveness of its educational system becomes critical. With such a development, language proficiency becomes even more essential to the success of cross-cultural communication and global participation. However, there are many apparent areas for improvement in language instruction, as presented currently in Vietnam, as many other factors such as competition and underqualified teachers complicate the matter. This article seeks to carefully look into the structural flaws in the educational model, particularly as they relate to language studies through the standards agreed upon by Decision No.2080/QD-Ttg published in 2017 by the Vietnamese Prime Minister.

Through a detailed analysis of components such as curriculum design, instructional strategies, institutional structures, and socio-cultural agents, this study aims to clarify the complex variables that lead to the subpar effectiveness of language instruction in Vietnam. Beyond simply identifying problems, the ultimate goal is to provide insightful analysis that could guide wise reforms and innovative interventions, creating an atmosphere that supports the effective teaching of language studies in the Vietnamese educational system.

Policy Overview

No. 2080/QĐ-TTg: DECISION on the approval of adjustments and additions to the Project entitled “Teaching and Learning Foreign Languages in the National Education System, Period 2017-2025”

Guideline:

In pursuit of enhancing foreign language education across the nation, the Vietnamese government has embarked on a comprehensive set of strategic initiatives, outlining the direction of these improvements. These actions, carefully designed and implemented, aim to elevate language teaching and learning at every level of the education system. Through this legislation, eight concise points have been created as an outline for the education system:

Making significant progress in the quality of foreign language instruction and learning at all educational levels and training programs, and promoting the teaching of foreign languages in social studies and early childhood education;

Encouraging the integration of teaching foreign languages (FLs) with other topics (such as math, science, and specialized majors), all while utilizing English as the primary language of instruction;

Encouraging the use of cutting-edge technologies in the teaching and learning of foreign languages by implementing e-learning platforms that are appropriate and available to everyone, enabling them to learn languages on any device, at any time, and with a focus on improving speaking and listening abilities;

Establish environments that support the learning of foreign languages in families, schools, and the community so that students, instructors, and family members can all study these languages together;

Assure the pedagogical competence and competency in foreign languages of foreign language instructors in schools and universities, as well as those who instruct in other topics and provide foreign language majors;

Enhance the capacity for national testing and assessment in the teaching and learning of foreign languages;

Provide prioritized support in improving foreign language teaching and learning in disadvantaged areas;

Encourage and publicize the use of foreign language centers for instruction and learning of foreign languages outside of the classroom;

Improve management of the Project to ensure its practicality, feasibility, and effectiveness.

Achieved

Making significant progress in the quality of foreign language instruction and learning at all educational levels and training programs, and promoting the teaching of foreign languages in social studies and early childhood education.

According to the annual report, the Ministry of Education and Training completed developing and disseminating the foreign language curricula for the 2018 general education program. To date, all 63 provinces and centrally controlled cities in the country have adopted the English curriculum for Grades 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, and 10. English language learning activities are available to preschoolers in 53 out of 63 provinces and centrally controlled cities for the 2022–2023 school year.

According to the survey, 84% of English language teachers in primary schools, 87% in secondary schools, and 77% in high schools in Vietnam meet the requirements for foreign language competency in order to implement the new foreign language teaching programs.

Encouraging the integration of teaching foreign languages (FLs) with other topics (such as math, science, and specialized majors), all while utilizing English as the primary language of instruction.

With its emphasis on a comprehensive approach to learning that incorporates foreign languages into core topics, the Revised General Education Curriculum (2018) represents a momentous milestone in Vietnam's educational environment. The goals, methods of implementation, and effects on the educational system of Vietnam's Revised General Education Curriculum (2018) have been examined in light of its critical role in advancing the country's efforts to integrate foreign languages into core curricula.

The 2018 Revised General Education Curriculum incorporates foreign languages into core disciplines in a variety of ways. First of all, it places a strong emphasis on the development of communicative competence in foreign languages, giving learning language skills like speaking, listening, reading, and writing first priority. This communicative method guarantees that language acquisition is useful and adaptable to actual circumstances.

The curriculum also encourages the use of real-world resources and activities that place language learning within the framework of fundamental topics. Teachers can construct meaningful learning experiences that engage students and encourage language acquisition by utilizing multimedia tools, authentic texts, and project-based activities.

Additionally, the curriculum promotes collaborative teaching methods in which language and subject matter experts collaborate to create cohesive lesson plans and educational activities. By ensuring that language learning objectives and subject-specific goals are in line, this cooperative method boosts the efficacy of integrated language training.

This Revised General Education Curriculum held its position as a significantly binding structure of the Vietnamese education system and showed the nation’s furthered steps toward the direction and aim that it has announced, especially within this sub-topic of decision No. 2080/QĐ-TTg.

Encourage and publicize the use of foreign language centers for instruction and learning of foreign languages outside of the classroom.

Under the 1986 Đổi Mới (Renovation) strategy, Vietnam underwent a period of economic and social transition that had a significant impact on many facets of society, including education. As Vietnam continues to integrate into the global world, there have been considerable improvements and advancements in language instruction in particular.

Bringing the growth of knowledge and spurts of learning outside of the classrooms, a wide variety of language centers have spread across the cities of Vietnam, differing with regard to scope, intended audience, pedagogy, and cost. Offering established curricula, state-of-the-art facilities, and licensed instructors, large multinational chains like Wall Street English, ILA Vietnam, and Apollo English have a substantial presence in key cities like Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Smaller independent language schools, on the other hand, concentrate on conversational skills or test preparation, catering to specialized markets or language demands. Furthermore, online platforms and apps have grown in popularity, offering busy professionals and students flexible and easy learning options.

Encouraging the use of cutting-edge technologies in the teaching and learning of foreign languages by implementing e-learning platforms that are appropriate and available to everyone, enabling them to learn languages on any device, at any time, and with a focus on improving speaking and listening abilities.

The current state of e-learning platforms for language learning in Vietnam is characterized by a wide range of options that cater to different needs and preferences. Prominent global platforms like Duolingo, Rosetta Stone, and Babbel are easily accessed and provide Vietnamese language learners with an extensive selection of language courses available in different languages. Furthermore, there exist locally created platforms and programs designed exclusively for Vietnamese language learners, offering Vietnamese-language information while emphasizing often-encountered foreign languages including English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean.

E-learning platforms in Vietnam are increasingly prioritizing the development of speaking and listening abilities through innovative features and interactive exercises, allowing learners to practice speaking in real-world contexts through virtual speaking partners and conversation simulations.

2. To be improved on/Unachieved

Establish environments that support the learning of foreign languages in families, schools, and the community so that students, instructors, and family members can all study these languages together.

A deeper look reveals a number of obstacles and constraints with the idea of creating settings that support foreign language acquisition in Vietnamese families, schools, and communities. Efforts to encourage language acquisition in diverse contexts are definitely underway, but the existing state of affairs in Vietnam poses challenges to their successful execution.

A centrally recurring theme in regards to this outlined incentive lies deep within the core values and beliefs of the Vietnamese society, though this is a means of generalizing or stereotyping, these views are those “traditional”, and evidently present amongst many citizens here.

In Vietnamese culture, the family plays a central role, and while familial support for education is valued, the emphasis is often placed on traditional subjects such as mathematics and sciences rather than foreign languages. Many families prioritize academic subjects perceived as directly contributing to career prospects and economic stability.

Even in cases opposing those above, not all families have the means or educational background to actively encourage learning a foreign language at home due to socioeconomic gaps, even if they were in agreement with the more open-minded views of language studies presented now.

Though there have been efforts of encouragement and promotion to create environments of engaged foreign language activity, the realistic outcomes prove feeble and unyielding, and a field much needed to be further invested in and improved on.

Assure the pedagogical competence and competency in foreign languages of foreign language instructors in schools and universities, as well as those who instruct in other topics and provide foreign language majors.

The importance of pedagogical competence in foreign language instruction cannot be overstated, especially in a country like Vietnam where language proficiency is increasingly valued for academic, professional, and personal advancement. In not only regions nearing this country but internationally as well, tests and degrees have been a long-standing method of certification of competence in specialized fields or expertise.

Though many of these tests are internationally accredited and used as an applicable form of criteria, it is extremely difficult to take into account the matter of language fluency and cogency in areas where a specific language is not common and used primarily, especially in the case of English.

In foreign language studies, education in these areas, writing, reading, and listening are the most commonly prioritized above all. This is mainly due to the “textbook” nature of these skills, where an instructor merely has to teach a rigid set of rules and patterns of said language. What these responsible individuals may lack just as much as their pupil does, is verbal language fluency. Once taking languages off of paper and into verbal dialogue, even the studied educator can have a hard time with their language flow, taking into account the difficulties in learning rhythmic patterns, intonation, and annunciation that one usually acquires from direct interactions.

This is primarily due to a lack of exposure to proper exchanges of the language, where additional senses are now needed instead of merely using sight to read up on a topic. With dialogue, you must listen and mutually converse with someone fluent in order to correctly learn.

Without this reassurance in correct pronunciation and tone, many educators, co mmonly those in the public education system, end up with a shot in the dark. There is no one around to correct them, and so misinformation and miscommunications are passed down to their students, who are also now adhering to improper verbal language.

Even if they are eloquent in all other skills needed in a language, speech remains one of the most, if not the most, crucial, seeing as language, at its core, is a means of communication, one that is most prevalently verbalized.

Enhance the capacity for national testing and assessment in the teaching and learning of foreign languages.

With a primary focus on evaluating English proficiency, Vietnam has constructed a national testing framework for foreign languages. The National Foreign Language Project 2020 (NFLT), the Cambridge English Qualifications, and standardized assessments like the TOEFL and IELTS are the most well-known English language exams. With the goal of evaluating students' speaking, listening, reading, and writing abilities, these tests offer standardized, nationally and internationally accepted measures of competency.

Yet, the foreign language tests that need to be discussed are those accredited only nationally, otherwise known as tests and exams made by the Vietnamese Ministry of Education and Training in the public education system. These tests are incorporated directly as a grading mechanism for public academic records, and so are developed and created nationally instead of having an international system of credit.

Despite the existence of standardized tests, several challenges persist in the assessment of foreign language proficiency in Vietnam. The emphasis on rote memorization and exam preparation, which might not fairly represent students' communicative ability or practical language abilities, is one major obstacle. Furthermore, differences between classroom instruction and test expectations might arise from an inconsistency between assessment techniques and pedagogical approaches, which can result in test-oriented teaching as opposed to meaningful language learning.

Provide prioritized support in improving foreign language teaching and learning in disadvantaged areas.

The current situation of foreign language teaching and learning can be defined of their lack of basic infrastructure and resources necessary for effective language education. Textbooks, teaching materials, and audio-visual resources essential for language learning are harder to come by for schools in rural and isolated locations. The difficulties these groups encounter are further compounded by the lack of certified language teachers, as many schools use inexperienced or underqualified teachers to teach foreign languages.

In these environments, a lot of families find it difficult to prioritize language education over more pressing financial issues or to finance additional language learning tools. Furthermore, migration patterns may interfere with kids' education, increasing the likelihood of dropouts and causing uneven access to language learning.

In response to this continued state, the Vietnamese government has put in place a number of initiatives and regulations targeted at enhancing language instruction in underprivileged communities. For instance, there are provisions in the National Target Program on New Rural Development for improving language instruction in rural areas. Furthermore, programs like the Vietnam-Korea Vocational Training College in the province of Lao Cai and the National Foreign Language Project 2020 have improved language education possibilities for pupils in underprivileged areas.

Conclusion

This examination of the Vietnamese education system's shortcomings in delivering language studies reveals a critical need for reform and innovation. The issues raised—such as outmoded instructional strategies, a lack of resources, and little exposure to real-world language use—highlight how urgently radical reform is needed. In order to address these problems, legislators, educators, parents, and students must work in collaboration.

Prioritizing student-centered strategies that encourage active learning, critical thinking, and linguistic competency in everyday situations is essential going forward. The effectiveness and quality of language instruction can be improved by making investments in technological integration, current teaching materials, and teacher training. In addition, encouraging language exchange and cultural immersion opportunities can broaden students' horizons and improve their language proficiency.
