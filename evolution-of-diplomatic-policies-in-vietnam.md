Evolution of Diplomatic Policies in Vietnam

Vietnam is a country that is extremely rich in history, having successfully defended itself against numerous invaders and secured alliances with many countries ever since its creation. Such development has also prompted the advancement of diplomatic policies in Vietnam, which are flexible and made to be well-fitted to whatever era they are in.

In wartime and the early nation-building era, Vietnam usually utilized diplomacy to plead for freedom and independence alongside military efforts. After the successful August revolution, President Ho Chi Minh became Vietnam’s first Minister of Foreign Affairs, and it was in these initial years of formation that the basic principles of Vietnamese diplomacy were laid out. The late President believed in a diplomatic approach that is “firm in objectives, flexible in strategies and tactics.” In addition, it is important to note that most of this era’s diplomacy was focused on the justification of Vietnamese independence and the prevention of French re-invasion and re-occupation. Thus, President Ho aligned the Vietnamese revolution with the revolutionary trend that was apparent in the world, while also preaching about amicability and friendliness with the international community, including all democratic countries (Nguyen V. L., 2022). This friendly attitude towards all nations was the start of Vietnam’s participation in the Non-Aligned Movement, which later became fundamental in its diplomatic policies. In the end, by joining the global cause of revolution and making additional efforts to fend off the occupation of the Nazi Japanese, Vietnam’s diplomatic plea for independence was able to appeal to the international community, including Allied countries, and secure support and recognition of independence from many of them. This success was felt in and made clear on the diplomatic table at the 1954 Geneva Conference, where the French colonization of Vietnam was resolved and abolished completely, all thanks to the clever diplomatic strategies of the Vietnamese diplomats: to stay firm with our plea for independence and utilize opportunities such as military victories while also considering other nations’ interests and allowing room for negotiation and compromise.

In the later period (from the resistance war to the pre-Doi Moi independence era), Vietnam emerged as a new and rural-centric country, operating diplomatically with the aid of its socialist allies only, which proved to be extremely limiting for the newly reunited nation. For starters, Vietnam’s strict conformance to the socialist bloc and ideology meant Vietnam was not able to seek new alliances outside of such a bloc. In fact, Vietnam placed its alliance with the Soviet Union at the cornerstone in this era, fostering a strong yet overdependent bond, both economically and diplomatically, with the Union. In addition, Vietnam’s involvement with the Khmer Rouge and the lasting effects of the resistance war further worsened the state of Vietnamese foreign affairs and diplomacy, as the international community placed severe repression policies and trade restrictions on the young nation. At the end of this period, Vietnam became a diplomatically isolated country in the international political scene. However, this is not to say that Vietnam did not achieve any diplomatic success at this time. One notable achievement was the Paris Peace Accords, which served as the agreement to end the military activities in Vietnam at the time and restore peace in the country. During this meeting, Nguyen Thi Binh, leader of the Vietnamese delegation, rose as an extremely well-spoken diplomatic figure, and later, became the only female signatory of the Peace Accords.

The isolationist era ended with the commencement of “Doi Moi.” After the aid withdrawal, and ultimately, the fall of the Soviet Union, Vietnam’s economy plunged deeply, while diplomacy, which in the past consisted of mostly socialist countries with the now dissolved Soviet Union being the center, suffered heavily. It was at that moment that the Vietnamese Communist Party decided it was time for a change, thus, the country became more open to the diversification of diplomatic ties with other nations. The highlight of this era is that Vietnam strived to make efforts to normalize relations with ex-Cold War rival countries, forming strategic alliances that proved to be beneficial to both sides, while also establishing new relations with countries worldwide.

Like most countries, diplomatic policies in Vietnam currently serve 02 main purposes: defending the nation’s sovereignty and cooperating with the world. According to the Diplomatic Academy of Vietnam, the 4 words used to describe this goal are independence, self-reliance, diversification, and multilateralization (Diplomatic Academy of Vietnam, 2022). Nowadays, Vietnam continues to follow the teachings of President Ho, thus staying firm in protecting national interests while being flexible in approaches, and this later becomes what is known as “bamboo diplomacy” (or “ngoại giao cây tre”). Moreover, having joined the Non-Aligned Movement officially in 1976, Vietnam established the “4 No’s” in diplomacy, which include: no military alliances; no siding with one country against another; no foreign military bases and no using Vietnamese territory to oppose other countries; and no using force or threatening to use force in international relations (Vietnam National Defence White Paper, 2019), and has continuously been utilizing such principles to navigate the global political scene amidst multiple conflicts and gradually gain respect and standing among other nations.

As of 2024, Vietnam has been a member of the United Nations for 47 years and has served as the President and non-permanent member of the United Nations Security Council. During its time in these international forums, Vietnam has made multiple contributions to the cause for peace and participated in several Working Groups and missions. The country also established strategic partnerships with other nations and had the honor of welcoming world leaders to its land, most remarkably Russian President Vladimir Putin, General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party Xi Jinping, and United States President Joe Biden. These are the fruits of Vietnam’s amicable diplomatic policies, which helped the country gradually become a well-supported and praised diplomatic player.

In the future, Vietnam is to maintain its “Bamboo Diplomacy” and Non-Aligned policy, while continuing to advance further on the world stage, extending the realms of its diplomatic relations further, potentially establishing full partnerships with more nations, and making more valuable suggestions in the international community. Vietnam has the potential to be a leader of its ASEAN bloc, a representative of peace, and an influential player in the diplomatic field worldwide.
